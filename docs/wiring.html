FDDSynthPro · the drive · firmware 0.6 pin map

Olimex BB48 to a 34-pin floppy header

Ten inputs straight to the BB48, each pulled up by the SIP network; five outputs through the SN7407, which pulls the cable low for the BB48. The SD card, the amp and the front panel hang off the other pins. Pick any pin or row to see its wire.

Use a plain BB48, not the BB48R

On the R, GPIO 8 is the PSRAM chip-select and GPIO 9–11 go to its SD slot. Here they carry MOTEB, DIR, STEP and WDATA.

Power order

BB48 on (USB-C) before the host's USB; the host off first. The RP2350's pins are 5 V tolerant only while it is powered, and the FeatherWing's outputs are 5 V.

Odd pins are all ground

Pins 1–33 odd. Wire at least five, spread along the header, to the ground rail; all seventeen is better. Pins 4 and 6 stay unconnected.

One strip per header pin

The box header's two columns must not share a Perma-Proto strip: cut the trace between them under every row (see the board plan), then check with a meter that pins 1 and 2 aren't connected.

5 V only to the SN7407 and the amp

From the BB48's VBUS (EXT2-1). Everything else runs at 3.3 V: the SIP common and the SD card from +3.3V (EXT1-1).

On the Perma-Proto

A plan by zones, not hole by hole: check it against the real parts before soldering. Rows run along the board; each half-row (a–e, f–j) is one strip.

Placing

  • BB48 across the centre channel at the far end (rows 34–60), in female headers so it can be swapped. EXT1 faces the header end: all fifteen floppy signals are on it.
  • Box header at the near end, on the left half, its two columns in holes c and d. Cut the trace between c and d under all 17 rows, so pin 1 and pin 2 (and every pair) are on separate strips.
  • SIP network in a 1 × 11 socket on the right half of the header's rows, one pin per row, pin 1 (the dot) nearest the end. Pin 1 to +3.3V; pins 2–11 to the ten inputs.
  • SN7407 in its socket across the centre channel, notch towards the header, with 100 nF between pins 14 and 7.
  • SD breakout in the rows between the SN7407 and the BB48, so its six SDIO wires to GPIO 32–37 are short and alike.

First power-up check

With the host connected and reset (gw reset --device <port>), type p in the serial monitor:

PINS DENSEL=OFF MOTEA=OFF DRVSB=OFF DRVSA=OFF MOTEB=OFF DIR=OFF STEP=OFF WDATA=OFF WGATE=OFF SIDE1=OFF
  • Every input OFF (high, on its pull-up). DENSEL may be either: it's a level, not a request.
  • An input stuck ON usually means a wire on the wrong pin, or the SIP in backwards.
  • Run the solder-bridge test first, cable unplugged: x then Y.
